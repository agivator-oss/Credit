Data Centres and AI

Industrial systems, energy constraints, government policy, and scenario modelling for AI-scale compute infrastructure.

HNglobal Pty Ltd · Report overview

The question

AI is pulling compute into the class of heavy industrial infrastructure. Data centres at this scale are no longer a back-office IT choice; they reshape electricity systems, water and land use, capital allocation, skills pipelines, and the options available to governments.

This report treats data centres and AI as a coupled industrial–policy problem: what gets built, where, under which rules, with what energy and resource bargain, and toward what national capability.

What the analysis covers

  • Systems view How compute, power, cooling, networks, and supply chains interact — and where bottlenecks actually bind.
  • Technology strategy Choices between hosting, sovereign capacity, specialised industrial AI, and dependency on offshore platforms.
  • Government policy Levers that matter: planning and permitting, grid connection, energy and water expectations, skills, and security.
  • Scenario modelling Plausible demand and delivery paths, including constrained build-out, accelerated lift-off, and efficiency-led trajectories.

Why it matters

Policymakers and industry leaders are being asked to approve or finance assets whose operating life spans decades, while AI demand, chip roadmaps, and energy markets move on much shorter cycles. Without a systems frame and explicit scenarios, decisions default to project-by-project negotiation — and national capability emerges by accident.

The report is written for people who need to decide — ministers, agency leads, infrastructure sponsors, and industrial strategists — not for slide volume.
